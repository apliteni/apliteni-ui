# Stat bands

How a screen shows key figures and explains changes beside them.

## Put key figures in a stat band, not a card of parts.

<!-- rule: a-band-not-a-card -->

**Do:** Use one card per figure by default. Make the figure the largest item. Show the change with an arrow, signed number, and one caption above the row stating what all changes are measured against.

**Don't:** The Overview card rejected in #267, rebuilt from the same kit parts. Placing the figure where the card title belongs makes key numbers look like headings. The change is a capitalized 10px status chip with no stated basis.

## Colour a change by whether it is good news, not by which way it points.

<!-- rule: tone-not-direction -->

**Why:** Neutral means the band gives no news verdict. Use it for a volume nobody scores, a figure whose good direction depends on the reader, or a figure already accounted for beside it. Net cashflow is income less cost; those figures already state whose news they are. An undeclared change still shows its arrow and states its basis; only colour is withheld. A caller can pass "neutral" explicitly, with the same result.

**Do:** Give each of the four figures its own verdict. Income and Cost both rose, but use opposite colours because rising income and rising cost are different news. Unclassified fell; fewer unclassified rows is good news, so colour the fall green. Net cashflow has no declared tone, so leave it grey.

**Don't:** Do not colour the four figures by sign. That makes three rises green, congratulating a year of cost growth, while colouring this year's cleanup work—the only item in red.

## Say what a change is measured against, in text the reader can reach.

<!-- rule: say-the-basis -->

**Why:** A percentage without a basis is a number nobody can check. The rejected card put its basis in a title attribute, which phones do not show and most screen readers skip. The band states it once in a caption every change points to, or beside the one change measured against something else. The caption comes before the row, like a table caption, so readers hear it before the numbers. It must be outside every figure: under tiles it sounds like a note for the last card; inside the first tile it sounds like that figure's comparison. A band fails the basis check if a change has neither a reachable basis nor a caption, or if the caption is inside a figure. Therefore this rule has no drawn don't.
