# The page

## Keep one shell

<!-- rule: layout -->

**Rule:** Use one shell layout throughout the product: either the rail alone or the rail with a bar above the page containing search and the reader’s menu.

**Why:** Readers learn where search and account controls are once.

**Do:** Use the same shell on every screen.

**Don't:** Move search or the account menu between pages.

## Group the sidebar one way

<!-- rule: sidebar -->

**Rule:** Give the sidebar group labels with no sub-items, or sub-items with no group labels, and never both.

**Why:** Both together make a long sidebar that scrolls, so the reader cannot see all the sections at once.

**Do:** Put Pending and History under Payouts, with no group labels.

**Don't:** Put Payouts and its sub-items under a Money label.

## Align the sidebar on one column

<!-- rule: sidebar-column -->

**Rule:** Align the logo, navigation icons, version, and avatar on one column, and hide the version when the sidebar folds.

**Why:** One edge leads the eye from the logo to the account, and a folded strip has no room for words.

**Do:** Put one fact, such as the version, above the account.

**Don't:** Start the version under the section names.

## Keep the bar’s end for product alerts

<!-- rule: bar-end -->

**Rule:** Put only glyph controls that serve every page, such as the notification bell, at the right end of the bar.

**Why:** The reader looks for the bell in the same place on every page, and a phone bar has room for two glyphs at most.

**Do:** Put the bell at the bar’s end, after search.

**Don't:** Put a page’s own button, such as New report, in the bar.

## Choose content width

<!-- rule: width -->

**Rule:** Use the wide column for mostly tables and boards, and the centred column for mostly reading and forms.

**Why:** Tables need space, while reading needs a line length readers can follow.

**Do:** Show every table column at once in the wide column.

**Don't:** Put the same table in the centred column when its last column goes off-screen.

## Order page headings

<!-- rule: head -->

**Rule:** Put the way back first, then the page title and a short introduction, with everything else below.

**Why:** Readers should know which page they are on before choosing filters.

**Do:** Back link → title → introduction → filters.

**Don't:** Put filters above the title.

**Except:** Small tools for the whole page, such as a version badge, a period picker or a view action, stand at the end of the title row.

## Use one page title

<!-- rule: one-h1 -->

**Rule:** Give each page exactly one page title.

**Why:** Readers using heading navigation need one clear place to land.

**Do:** Use one h1 for the page.

**Don't:** Use multiple page titles or none.

## Follow heading levels

<!-- rule: outline -->

**Rule:** Use headings in order without skipping levels, and stop at h3: page, section, then group.

**Why:** Skipped levels hide structure, and a fourth level usually means the page should be split.

**Do:** Use a page heading, then section headings, then group headings.

**Don't:** Skip from the page heading to a group heading, or add a fourth heading level.

## Emphasize one action

<!-- rule: one-primary -->

**Rule:** Use one filled button for the main action and give all other actions less emphasis.

**Why:** Equal emphasis makes the main action unclear.

**Do:** Make Create token primary, with Import agents and Export less prominent.

**Don't:** Give all three actions equal emphasis.

## Never nest cards

<!-- rule: stacking -->

**Rule:** Never put a card inside another card.

**Why:** Two borders around one thing give the inner card no rank, so the reader cannot tell which box holds the content.

**Do:** Stack the cards at one level, as many as the page needs.

**Don't:** Put a card inside another card.

## Leave pages closed

<!-- rule: at-rest -->

**Rule:** Show the page uncovered until the reader chooses to open something.

**Why:** An overlay on arrival interrupts reading and can move keyboard focus.

**Do:** Let the reader open a menu or dialog.

**Don't:** Open one automatically when the page loads.

**Except:** When a session ends while the reader works, open a sign-in dialog over the page, keep its shell and title, and replace its data with skeleton shapes until sign-in.

## Keep table density consistent

<!-- rule: density -->

**Rule:** Use compact rows in every table on the page, or use compact rows in none of them.

**Why:** Consistent row heights make tables feel like one page.

**Do:** Use compact rows in both tables.

**Don't:** Use compact rows in one table and roomy rows in another.

**Except:** Use the spacing required by the busiest table.

## Brand the background behind a dialog page

<!-- rule: branded-background -->

**Rule:** Give each product its own branded background behind a dialog-only page.

**Why:** The kit's one default gradient does not distinguish a product.

**Do:** Set a product colour or a slow-moving gradient behind the dialog. The kit default stays otherwise.

**Don't:** Put no field, label or button on the branded ground. Keep the task inside the dialog.

**Except:** A wordmark and a slogan may stand on the ground. A dialog clears 3:1 against its ground in both themes, except the sign-in and Consent ground C, which the owner chose at 1.60:1 to 1.51:1 light and 1.08:1 to 1.04:1 dark.

## The sign-in note names who can sign in

<!-- rule: sign-in-note -->

**Rule:** The sign-in note states who can sign in without naming the product.

**Why:** A product name ties the note to one product, and the note breaks when another app uses the screen.

**Do:** Write "Only work accounts can sign in."

**Don't:** Name the product or its domain in the note.

## Write a useful introduction

<!-- rule: lede -->

**Rule:** A page may open on its title alone; where there is an introduction, keep it to two sentences or fewer and add information the title does not give.

**Why:** Repeating the title wastes the reader’s time.

**Do:** Below “Payouts”, say “Payouts arrive within three business days.”

**Don't:** Write “This is the payouts page” below “Payouts,” or keep a line there because the page looks bare without one.
