# Dashboards and reports

## Build a dashboard for one screen

<!-- rule: fast-glance -->

**Rule:** Build a dashboard to answer “is anything wrong, and what changed” in one screen, without scrolling.

**Why:** A reader opens a dashboard between other work, so whatever sits below the fold is not part of the answer. Nothing is drawn here because the rule is about a viewport, and a specimen cell cannot set one.

**Except:** A phone stacks the same blocks and scrolls. Budget the content against a desktop screen rather than a phone.

**Do:** One period control, one row of figures and one short list of exceptions.

**Don't:** A second screen of blocks a reader has to scroll past the figures to reach.

## Put only figures, trends and exceptions on a dashboard

<!-- rule: figures-and-exceptions -->

**Rule:** Keep a dashboard to figures, their trends and the rows that need attention.

**Why:** A full table and a written note both ask a reader to read, and reading is what a report is for.

**Do:** Three figures with their trends, then the payouts that need a decision.

**Don't:** The whole ledger and a paragraph about it, on the page meant for a glance.

## Link every item to its report

<!-- rule: link-to-the-report -->

**Rule:** Link every item on a dashboard to the report that explains it.

**Why:** A dashboard stops at what happened, so without the link a reader who wants why has to go looking for it.

**Except:** Give the report its own place in the navigation as well, so a reader who already knows the question reaches it without the dashboard.

**Do:** Each reference opens that row in the report, and one link under the block opens the whole report.

**Don't:** The same references as plain text, with nothing on the block that opens the report.

## Build a report for the whole answer

<!-- rule: report-depth -->

**Rule:** Build a report to answer “why, and exactly how much”, with every row and every breakdown behind the figure.

**Why:** Analysis needs the rows themselves, because a summary of them cannot say how much.

**Do:** The payout ledger in full, down to fees and the net each payout settled.

**Don't:** The same ledger reduced to three figures, so the amounts a reader came for are gone.

## Give a report its filters and its export

<!-- rule: report-offers -->

**Rule:** Offer filters and one export on every report.

**Why:** A reader narrowing the question, or taking the rows into a spreadsheet, should not have to ask for another screen.

**Do:** A filter row and one export above the table.

**Don't:** A table alone, its period fixed by whoever built the page.

## Put the table under every chart

<!-- rule: table-behind-the-chart -->

**Rule:** Show the table a chart was drawn from under the chart, on every report.

**Why:** A chart carries the shape of a change and never the amount, and a report is read for the amount.

**Do:** The months as bars, then the same months as rows.

**Don't:** The bars alone, leaving a reader to estimate each month by eye.
