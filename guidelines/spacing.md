# Spacing

## Use two gaps

<!-- rule: two-gaps -->

**Rule:** Use the inner gap between parts of one group, and use the group gap between groups.

**Why:** Two distances show which parts belong together, and the reader never has to compare a third distance.

**Do:** Treat a control row and the table that it filters as one group.

**Don't:** Add a third distance between sections.

## Widen inner gaps on a touch phone

<!-- rule: two-gaps-phone -->

**Rule:** On a touch phone, make the inner gaps equal to the gap of the chip rows.

**Why:** The chip rows open for a finger, so a smaller gap makes the table look like part of the last row.

**Do:** Move the table down together with the chips.

**Don't:** Keep the desktop gap of the table.

**Except:** The view strip stays tight because its tabs meet the floor.

## Keep a title on its block

<!-- rule: title -->

**Rule:** At every width, keep a page title or a card title one inner gap above the content that it names.

**Why:** If a title has space on both sides, the title can seem to belong to either block.

**Do:** Keep the title of a card close to its first line.

**Don't:** Put a title halfway between two groups.

## Put text on the column edge

<!-- rule: text-edge -->

**Rule:** If a control draws no box, its first label starts on the edge of the page title.

**Why:** If nothing is drawn around a label, an indent makes the label look like a misaligned line.

**Do:** Let the highlight and the ring of the view strip extend into the margin.

**Don't:** Move a filter chip or a table away from the column edge.
